Mohari Villas / complex geometries

Eight hillside villas by Zaha Hadid Architects on the Papagayo Peninsula, each under a timber shell that rises from a single trunk. I was on the architect-of-record team that made the shells buildable.

Place
Papagayo, Costa Rica
Context

Contribution

A closer look

The Mohari villas are eight houses by Zaha Hadid Architects on the Papagayo Peninsula, each under timber shells that rise from a single trunk like a giant mushroom. On the architect-of-record team in Costa Rica, with Garnier Arquitectos and MAZ, I coordinated the design architects, the local consultants and the facade consultant through design development.

My work sat where the geometry met the documents: developing the shell form, writing the Grasshopper scripts that planarised the glazing and broke it into fabricable panels, and carrying the model across Rhino, Revit and the drawing set.

The canopy in design development

Villa 555: the stairs

From the live model to Revit.

The shells came from ZHCODE, the computational group at Zaha Hadid Architects, as a live Grasshopper model: a mesh, a planar grid, centrelines with their IDs, construction planes and section profiles for every glulam beam.

In July 2022 I carried villa 346 from that model toward the construction model. The output was baked to 5,403 curves and lines, and the canopy linework was split by layer, 5,444 curves in all, so Dynamo could read it from CAD layers, frame it and keep the geometry in sync with its Revit families.

Villa 346, July 2022: the live model into Revit, in five steps

Each canvas is shown whole, with a box on the part that does the step, and then that part. Open either to zoom.

01

The live model

ZHCODE delivered each canopy as a live Grasshopper model, one group per canopy, ending in its construction planes, section profiles and beam geometry.

02

My pass

I baked villa 346 out of the live model and split the canopy linework by layer: centrelines, hoops and section marks, each on its own.

03

Layers to curves

Dynamo read the linked drawing by layer name and drew each canopy layer in Revit as model curves, with bimorphNodes.

04

Curves to beams

Each curve became a structural beam, set on its level and given its glulam framing type.

05

Solids to families

The solids came in from SAT files and synced as Revit families, set up once in the input group, so Revit could schedule and draw them.

My working models

Villa 328: the glass, unit by unit.

The facade of villa 328 was set out in Rhino as a curtain system on a 1.5 by 3 m grid, with its columns and glass lines, then every insulated glass unit was measured and scheduled: 32 in the main space and 21 in the den, no two the same size. The opaque panels were scheduled in the same pass, by volume and length.
