Tamarindo 2050 / water as an urban framework

My thesis: a plan for Tamarindo drawn from its water. The new grid follows the runoff of the hills, so the town gathers rain through the dry season and makes room for it in the wet one.

Place
Tamarindo, Costa Rica
Context
Thesis, Universidad Veritas · 2017
Contribution
Author: the morphological and runoff analysis of the watershed, the recalibrated urban grid, and a public building that collects, stores and treats its own water

A closer look

Fast, speculative growth has left the Pacific coast without the infrastructure to manage its water. The project reads the land first: blocks sit between the drainage lines, open space follows the water, and the building closes the cycle.

The investigation treats infrastructure as a starting point for design. It connects regional patterns with the scale of an architectural intervention and forms an early thread in my interest in computational site analysis.

The runoff and slope definition

Rain is followed down the terrain: 1,303 points scattered over it are moved down and pulled back onto the surface, 200 times in a loop, and their paths are the runoff. The same mesh is coloured by its slope.

Reading the water

The street network

The building closes the cycle.

  1. A green roof catches the rain, slows it and filters it.
  2. Steel downpipes in the columns carry it to eight 50,000-litre tanks under the ground.
  3. Gravity takes it on to a constructed lagoon for biological treatment.
  4. It is pumped back into the building for use.
  5. Greywater runs down the natural slope into a two-stage filter.
  6. Filtered greywater joins the rainwater for final purification.

Slope, sun and water
