Capital Group / an elliptical stair

An elliptical stair through three floors of a Capital Group office in New York, from sketch to steel. Built.

Place
New York City, USA
Context
Gensler New York · built, 2018
Contribution
Job captain for the stair: its complete construction document set, the parametric script that set out the elliptical run, and coordination with the consultants and the fabricator through construction

A closer look

The stair is the one move in a quiet, three-storey workplace: a single elliptical run, wrapped in a brass guardrail, that ties the floors together around the lobby.

I produced the complete construction document set for the stair, wrote the parametric script that set out its elliptical run, and coordinated with the consultants and the fabricator through construction.

The stair in plan

The stair stands in the same place on every floor, beside the reception and the cafe, so each floor reads as a landing.

The set-out in four steps

Each canvas is shown whole, with a box on the groups that do the step, and then those groups. Open either to zoom.

01

From the height to the risers

The floor-to-floor height is divided by the maximum riser, 7 inches, and rounded up: that is the number of risers. The riser is the height over that number, the treads are one fewer, and the run on the walkline is the treads times an 11-inch going.

02

The run finds its own end

The walkline is an ellipse. Measured along it, the length of the run gives the point where the stair stops, so a taller floor carries the stair further round. The run is then divided into equal goings.

03

Edges, treads and risers

The walkline sits 12 inches from the inner edge. Both edges are offset from it, each division is carried across to them as a tread line, and each tread is lifted one riser above the last.

04

Guard, handrail and the table

The nosings along each edge make the stringers. The handrail follows them 42 inches up, with a baluster every 4 inches, and the last group lists every nosing, inner and outer, in x, y and z for the fabricator.
